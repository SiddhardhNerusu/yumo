## 1.0 build 2026-10-21 pm (fibre is not a carb)
- **Typing is quick again.** Measured on the simulator (Debug build): the search ran 102 ms per keystroke, all of it matching each typed word against every word of every row with prefix and edit-distance checks. Each typed word's matches are now worked out once per query over the ~6,000-word vocabulary, so scoring a row is dictionary lookups: 11 ms. The main thread was never the problem (keystroke 1–9 ms, results render 16 ms). Debounce 120 → 80 ms, so results land about 90 ms after you stop typing instead of 220. Rankings unchanged. - **Every row now follows the UK convention** (Sid: "don't count fibre as carbs"): carbs exclude fibre, and energy is protein × 4 + fat × 9 + carbs × 3.75 + alcohol × 7, with fibre counting nothing. That is exactly how the UK tables compute their own calories (99 % of CoFID rows within 3 % of that formula; fibre at 2 kcal/g fits only 77 %). USDA's carbohydrate is "by difference" and includes fibre, and its energy mixes conversion factors — 2,716 USDA rows are converted in `tools/build-catalogue.py`, 2,485 of them change: median −4 % kcal, e.g. dry-roasted almonds 598 → 595 kcal and 21 → 10 g carbs, chickpeas 164 → 133 kcal, broccoli 31 → 28, bananas 89 → 83; bran and dried fungi drop most (corn bran 224 → 67, it is 79 % fibre); a few rise where USDA had discounted energy (oat bran 246 → 323, lemon juice 22 → 28). Protein and fat never change.
- 245 USDA rows with carbs but no fibre figure (Foundation rows such as Hass avocado, apple juice, some canned beans) cannot be converted and keep USDA's carbs; they carry a `fibre unknown` flag in the catalogue. The Food page's source explainer says which convention a row uses.
- The builder's Atwater cross-check uses the UK formula now (5 rows flagged, was 225 under 4/4/9 — the mismatch was the fibre).

## 1.0 build 2026-10-19 evening (one search engine)
- **One scorer ranks every list** — Add food (Your foods · Generic · Chains · Scanned · Branded), I prepped…, Staples, and Open Food Facts results — built on the standard retrieval recipe. Coverage first: a row that names more of the typed words always ranks above one that names fewer, and a row must name at least half of them to show (so "tempura chicken chunks" by a brand called Rooster never answers "red rooster potato albert"). Then each word scores match quality (exact 1.0 · prefix 0.85, 0.6 while a 1–2 letter word is still being typed · fuzzy 0.5 — same first letter, one edit up to 7 letters) × rarity (log(1 + N / df), so "rooster" outweighs "potato") × position (the first word is the head noun, 1.5×); brand words count the same as name words. Bonuses: the whole line as a name prefix +2, contained +1, your history log(1 + useCount) × 1.2, raw rows +0.4, −0.02 per character of name so "Potatoes" beats "Sweet Potato Fries - Albert Bartlett" on a tie. Plurals are stemmed ("potatoes" = "potato"). 1–3 ms per query over the 4,160-row catalogue, off the main thread.
- Typed brand / variety words stay in the query next to their nutritional fallback ("rooster" → rooster, potato, red): the catalogue matches the fallback, a packaged product matches the word, both through the same rule. Words no catalogue row carries make the **Branded · Open Food Facts group lead** (in Add food and I prepped…) and trigger a second lookup on those words alone.
- Add food: live Open Food Facts search now runs automatically whenever the line names a brand or a word the catalogue can't cover (before: only on a known brand token or the "Show branded" tap).
- **Packaged rows now show what tells two bags apart**: the front-of-pack photo (40 pt) and the pack size next to the brand ("Albert Bartlett · 2 kg"), in Add food and I prepped…; the pack size is kept on the saved food as its detail line. Records that are identical in name, brand, size and nutrition (one product entered twice) collapse to one row. Open Food Facts doesn't record the variety for either Albert Bartlett bag, so the app does not claim one is Rooster — scanning the bag is the exact route.
- Open Food Facts lookups run per uncovered word, concurrently, merged by barcode (`searchMerged`): "rooster albert" as a pair buries the bags under "Roosters" chicken; "albert" alone surfaces them.
- Add food's branded lookup is part of the one search task for one captured line, so a keystroke cancels every stale lookup (before, a lookup for "alber" could land after the one for "albert" and win). Open Food Facts name searches are cached 10 min per word; when the service doesn't answer the row says so instead of "no match".
- Verified on the simulator: "red rooster potato albert" → Potatoes · Albert Bartlett (both bags), Irish Rooster Potatoes · Tesco, then their sweet potatoes and fries, then every catalogue red-potato row.
